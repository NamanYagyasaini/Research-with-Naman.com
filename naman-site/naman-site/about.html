Biography

Naman Srivastava is an M.Sc. Physics student (Class of 2024–2026) at NIT Silchar, India. His thesis, "Quantum Entanglement Measures of Cosmological Squeezed States in Starobinsky Inflation," uses the Mukhanov–Sasaki formalism to study quantum correlations generated during inflation. He completed a summer research internship at IIT Guwahati working on dark matter phenomenology — specifically thermal freeze-out and relic density calculations.

Outside of research, he is building physics education content aimed at JEE and NEET aspirants, and is an early-stage Himalayan travel photographer and content creator based in Lucknow, India.

Philosophy

Research Philosophy

Physics is most convincing when the formalism is grounded in physical intuition first. I try to work outward from a concrete question — what does entanglement actually measure here, physically? — before reaching for machinery, and to keep the two in conversation throughout a calculation.

Direction

Research Vision & Motivation

I'm motivated by open questions at the boundary of quantum information and cosmology: how entanglement measures constrain what we can infer about the early universe, and what quantum-information tools — simulation, error correction — can bring to problems in fundamental physics.

Near-term

Career Objective

Pursue a Ph.D. in theoretical physics, with a primary focus on quantum information and computing — entanglement structure, quantum simulation of many-body systems, and quantum error correction — and nuclear/particle physics as a secondary application area.

Long-term

Future Goals

Build an independent research program connecting quantum information methods to open problems in cosmology and fundamental physics, while continuing to develop accessible physics education content.
